The loop

You can't think your way out of your head.

You've tried. Of course you have. You solve problems for a living.

But thinking has no finish line. It loops. The same three thoughts in a different order, and no conclusion at the end.

Here's what most men try, and where it stalls:

  • Thinking it over one more timeMore loops. Still no conclusion.
  • Books and podcastsSmart, interesting, and about somebody else's life. Nothing changed.
  • A journalA blank page and no idea what to write. It felt pointless.
  • Pro and con listsYou stared at the list and still couldn't pick.
  • Talking to your partner"Just decide" is not a method.
  • Therapy or coachingFeels like it's for people with "real" problems. Too slow, too expensive, too exposing.

None of that is on you. You were handed more input. What you needed was a place where a thought can finish.

Why a pen

On paper, a thought has a beginning and an end.

In your head a thought can run for years. On paper it takes four lines.

Then it's there. Outside you. Where you can look at it, question it, and see where it's wrong.

That is all journaling is here: a thinking tool. Not a diary. Not emotional dumping. Nobody is going to ask how that makes you feel.

The method

Three steps. In this order.

Treat it like a case about your own life. First the evidence. Then the verdict. Then you carry it out.

01

Witness

Get the evidence

You see what is going on, in your life and in your week. A witness reports what happened. He doesn't interpret it. No opinions yet. Facts, on paper.

02

Analyze

Reach the verdict

You turn what you saw into choices you make yourself. Not what you're supposed to want. Not what someone tells you. Your call, in your own words.

03

Act

Carry it out

You follow through. A choice is worth nothing until it's carried out. So it gets a first move and a date.

Each step leans on the one before. No analysis without evidence. No action without a verdict. And once you've acted, you witness again: what moved, what didn't.

It's a loop, and you learn to run it yourself. No coach. No calls. No group. After 21 days you'll be able to do this alone, for the rest of your life.

Who made this

I'm Thijs. I picked up a pen.

I'm a Dutchman living in the Portuguese countryside, and I've kept a journal for 20 years. Sometimes consistently. More often not.

It took me most of those years to notice the pattern. In the periods I wrote, I was sharper and more confident. I made bigger choices. I got things done.

Then came one week in 2025. My wife and I signed the mortgage on a new house. I lost my job. And we got the news that our daughter needed intensive medical treatment.

Same week.

I picked up a pen. Not as therapy. To stay clear, and to choose deliberately while everything moved at once.

On paper I chose freedom and independence for my family. That's how we ended up here, and it's what I built Journaling Man from.

I'm not selling a miracle. You can lead a horse to water. This method works when you do it, and only then. I can give you the steps and the tools. The pen is yours.

Questions

Fair questions. Short answers.

Is this therapy?

No. It's a method for thinking and choosing on paper. It is not a substitute for professional help.

I've never journaled.

That's who it's built for. Every sitting tells you what to write.

I tried journaling and quit.

Most journals give you a blank page or a rigid form. This gives you a method, with a purpose for each sitting. You always know why you're writing and what you'll have when you're done.

How much time does it take?

About 15 minutes on the days you write. In the first three weeks there are four longer sittings of roughly an hour each. After 90 days you do one review sitting, and you repeat that every quarter.

Do I have to write every day?

No. At least three times a week does the job.

Is it only a PDF?

Yes. Workbooks you print or copy into your notebook, and reuse every quarter. No app, no login, no videos to sit through.

Do I need the Notion board?

No. It's optional. The method runs on a notebook and a pen.

Is there a group or calls?

No. This is private work. You, a notebook, and what's true.

Will I read it and do nothing, like the books?

Fair. A book asks you to read. This asks you to write, and each sitting ends with something on paper: a map, a direction, a choice, a first move with a date. But I won't pretend otherwise: it only works if you pick up the pen.
